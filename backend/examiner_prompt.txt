<misconceptions>
    <concept_1_misconceptions>
        1.1 Student confuses the mean with median or mode.
        1.2 Student believes the mean is the data for every individual observation rather than serving as a single prediction for all observations.
        1.3 Student believes that models are only visual representations of data
        1.4 Student thinks a “model” necessarily requires multiple variables or complexity, so they don’t recognize that a single number (the mean) can already function as a model.
    </concept_1_misconceptions>

    <concept_2_misconceptions>
        2.1 Student thinks data and model are the same thing.
        2.2 Student thinks if the model is “good,” every observation should equal the model.
        2.3 Student does not understand what each component of DATA = MODEL + ERROR represents.
    </concept_2_misconceptions>

    <concept_3_misconceptions>
        3.1 Student thinks error means a mistake, measurement error, or incorrect data rather than the difference between observed and predicted values.
        3.2 Student thinks error is something to eliminate completely rather than an inevitable consequence of the data generating process.
        3.3 Student thinks positive and negative errors indicate “good” versus “bad” observations rather than direction of deviation.
        3.4 Student does not understand why the residuals from the mean model sum to zero, expecting the positive and negative residuals to produce a nonzero total.
    </concept_3_misconceptions>

    <concept_4_misconceptions>
        4.1 Student thinks the empty model contains no prediction because it is “empty”.
        4.2 Student does not understand that the empty model produces the same predicted value (the mean) for every observation.
        4.3 Student struggles to see the empty model as a general rule/function, and thinks of it only in the context of the specific dataset it was computed from.
    </concept_4_misconceptions>
