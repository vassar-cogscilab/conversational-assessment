<clusters>
    # Understand data = model + error
    ## Cluster 1: Understand concept of statistical models
    - Map DATA = MODEL + ERROR in different contexts - Ch. 5-9
    - Define and use the concept of a statistical model as a function that produces a predicted score for each observation - Ch. 5, 7-9
    - Differentiate between the empty model and a more complex model - Ch. 5, 7-9
    - Identify the most appropriate model to use based on how variables are measured (e.g., group versus regression models) - Ch. 5,7-9
    - Distinguish between a model of data and a model of DGP - Ch. 5-9
    - Identify that the mean acts as a balancing point for (error) residuals in a distribution - Ch. 5
    - Recognize that the best fitting model minimizes the appropriate measure of error (in this course, sum of squares; SS) - Ch. 6-9
    - Understand the concept of “overfitting” a model - Ch. 8

    ## Cluster 2: Understand concept of error
    - Visually intuit/ compare which models or distributions show more error - Ch. 5-9
    - Define, calculate, and reason about residuals from models - Ch. 5-9
    - Recognize that residuals aggregate to measures of error - Ch. 6-9
    - Know that error from the empty model is all of the unexplained variation - Ch. 5-6
    - Quantify aggregate error as Sum of Absolute Deviations (SAD), Sum of Squares (SS), variance, and standard deviation, and interpret these measures (R; e.g., supernova) - Ch. 6
    - Recognize the pros and cons of different methods of quantifying error - Ch. 6

    ## Cluster 3: Understand what error explains about the model
    - Know that sum of squares from the empty model (SS total) can be partitioned into error and model sums of squares - Ch. 7-9
    - Recognize that SS total is determined by the outcome variable and so total variation for an outcome variable will be the same regardless of explanatory variables included in the model - Ch. 7-9
    - Interpret visual representations of SS error, SS model, and SS total from empty, group, and regression models - Ch. 7-9
    - Use SS, PRE, and F statistic to compare models appropriately - Ch. 7-9
    - Calculate (e.g., find ANOVA tables; R) and interpret PRE and F statistic (R) - Ch. 7-9
    - Explain the concept of degrees of freedom - Ch. 8-9

<rubric>
    <Clarity>
        High - Student uses precise language with unambiguous meaning.
        Low - Student’s response is vague or ambiguous.
        Irrelevant - Student’s answer is unrelated to the question.
    </Clarity>

    <Concept_Understanding>
        High - Student accurately describes the concept with correct application and terminology.
        Partial - Student shows some understanding with minor errors. Understanding is based on rote memorization and is inflexible.
        Low - Student misdefines, confuses, or misunderstands the concept.
    </Concept_Understanding>

    <Reasoning_Quality>
        High — Student explains the proof for their answer. They connect ideas logically and can justify their thinking.
        Medium — Student provides incomplete, circular, or only partially justified reasoning.
        Low — Student gives an answer with no explanation, or the reasoning is irrelevant/illogical.
    </Reasoning_Quality>

    <Transfer>
        - Knowledge transfer questions make the user apply initial knowledge to a new domain. 
        - Knowledge transfer problems vary along the following dimensions in terms of content:
            - Learned skill
                - Procedure: Transfer of a learned sequence of steps or calculations, executing it correctly when the situation maps onto a familiar format.
                - Principle: Transfer of the underlying idea itself, invoking it and adapting it to the new situation (can explain why it applies, not just what to do).
                - Other: Transfer of content that is neither a step-based procedure nor a general principle (eg, a form of representation, or another type of learned skill). 
        - Knowledge transfer problems vary from near to far along the following dimensions in terms of context
            - Knowledge domain:
                - Near: Apply the concept in statistical contexts similar to those encountered during learning.
                - Far: Apply the same concept to different subjects, the less quantitative, the farther the transfer.
        - Deduce and specify student’s transfer capabilities by evaluating student answer to changes in the transfer question domain.
            - The farther the context, the higher a correct answer is weighed for determining deep understanding. 
            - The nearer the context, the higher an incorrect answer is weighed for determining a lack of deep understanding.
            - Procedure evaluation: whether a procedure transfer problem was answered correctly.
            - Principle evaluation: whether a principle transfer problem was answered correctly.
        - Note not all questions and responses involve transfer problems, indicate this with “irrelevant”.
    </Transfer>
</rubric>
