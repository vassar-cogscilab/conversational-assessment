<rubric>
    <Clarity>
        High - Student uses precise language with unambiguous meaning.
        Low - Student’s response is vague, with meaning that cannot be reasonably inferred.
        Irrelevant - Student’s answer is unrelated to the question.
        Typo - Student’s answer appears to be an accidental, incomplete submission (e.g., cut off mid-word or mid-thought, sent before the student finished typing) rather than a deliberate, complete response.
    </Clarity>

    <Concept_Understanding>
        Correct - Student accurately responds to the tested concept with correct application, using their own phrasing.
        Partial - Student responds to the tested concept with minor errors. Understanding is based on rote memorization.
        Low - Student misdefines, confuses, or misunderstands the concept.
    </Concept_Understanding>

    <Reasoning_Quality>
        High — Student connects ideas logically and can explain their thinking. Their answer is supported.
        Medium — Student’s answer has obvious gaps in explanation or circular logic
        Low — Student’s answer does not respond to the question, or the reasoning is irrelevant.
        Irrelevant - The question does not require students to explain their reasoning.
    </Reasoning_Quality>

    <Transfer>
        - Knowledge transfer questions make the user apply initial knowledge to a new domain. 
        - Knowledge transfer problems vary along the following dimensions in terms of content:
            - Learned skill
                - Procedure: Transfer of a learned sequence of steps or calculations, executing it correctly when the situation maps onto a familiar format.
                - Principle: Transfer of the underlying idea itself, invoking it and adapting it to the new situation (can explain why it applies, not just what to do).
                - Other: Transfer of content that is neither a step-based procedure nor a general principle (eg, a form of representation, or another type of learned skill). 
        - Knowledge transfer problems vary from near to far along the following dimensions in terms of context
            - Knowledge domain:
                - Near: Apply the concept in statistical contexts similar to those encountered during learning.
                - Far: Apply the same concept to different subjects, the less quantitative, the farther the transfer.
        - Deduce and specify student’s transfer capabilities by evaluating student answer to changes in the transfer question domain.
            - The farther the context, the higher a correct answer is weighed for determining deep understanding. 
            - The nearer the context, the higher an incorrect answer is weighed for determining a lack of deep understanding.
            - Procedure evaluation: whether a procedure transfer problem was answered correctly.
            - Principle evaluation: whether a principle transfer problem was answered correctly.
        - Note not all questions and responses involve transfer problems, indicate this with “irrelevant”.
    </Transfer>
</rubric>
