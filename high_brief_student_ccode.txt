    - A model is anything that produces a predicted value for an observation. A single number, like the mean, already qualifies — a model doesn't need multiple variables or an equation.
    - DATA = MODEL + ERROR: the model supplies one predicted score, applied to every observation; error is whatever is left over between that prediction and the actual data point.
    - Error is not a mistake, and not measurement error — it's the expected, unavoidable gap between a prediction and a real observation, present even when the model is the right one to use.
    - Positive and negative error indicate direction of deviation from the prediction, not "good" versus "bad" observations.
    - The mean (the empty model) predicts the exact same value for every observation, including ones not yet seen — it's a general rule, not just a description of the sample it came from.
    - Residuals from the mean sum to zero because the mean is the data's balancing point.
